What this shows:
The significant counts with stat ranking now match S1 closely in three comparisons: 5 vs. 4, 27 vs. 25, and 17 vs. 16. POA 14d is the exception (5 vs. 10).
PH 4m, the paper’s headline comparison, reproduces strongly (0.92).
But the NES correlations are lower than yesterday’s 30-sample run in three of four comparisons. That fits yesterday’s SV1 finding: the 30-sample run used the authors’ own SV1 values, while the 33-sample run uses our recomputed SV1, which pulls results away from theirs. So the right samples with an approximate SV1 can match S1 less closely than slightly wrong samples with their exact SV1. SV1 matters more than the 3 samples.
PH 14d stays low (0.30), as expected, since S1’s PH 14d didn’t match any of their own DESeq2 comparisons either.
