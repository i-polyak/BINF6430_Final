I predicted wrong. I expected all four to come in lower because of the missing controls. Instead we found more DEGs at 14 days and fewer at 4 months. So fewer controls didn’t simply shrink everything, and the story is more interesting:

The paper’s main pattern holds: more DEGs at 4 months than at 14 days, in both regions.
But it’s much weaker for us. In the paper, PH goes 13 → 82 (about 6x) and POA 14 → 70 (5x). For us, PH goes 19 → 56 (3x) and POA 30 → 55 (under 2x). The “delayed response” signal, the paper’s headline finding, comes out noticeably flatter.

Likely contributors, worth listing for Friday but not yet confirmed:
Missing controls. Removing 3 intact samples changes the baseline every comparison is measured against, not just the statistical power. That can push counts in either direction.
Gene-type source. The authors labeled genes using current Ensembl (biomaRt) with version numbers stripped. Genes Ensembl has since retired or reclassified would drop out of their protein-coding count but stay in ours. That could explain part of the gap.
SV1 was estimated on 33 samples and we use it on 30.

gene_check <- do.call(rbind, lapply(names(paper_genes), function(n) {
  r <- res_list[[n]]
  do.call(rbind, lapply(paper_genes[[n]], function(g) {
    hits <- r[which(r$gene_name == g), ]
    if (nrow(hits) == 0) return(data.frame(comparison = n, gene = g, log2FC = NA, padj = NA))
    best <- hits[order(hits$padj), ][1, ]
    data.frame(comparison = n, gene = g, log2FC = round(best$log2FoldChange, 2), padj = signif(best$padj, 2))
  }))
}))
gene_check$significant <- !is.na(gene_check$padj) & gene_check$padj < 0.05

gene_check
table(gene_check$comparison, gene_check$significant)
