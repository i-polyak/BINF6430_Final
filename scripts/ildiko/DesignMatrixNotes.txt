DESeq2 builds the design matrix itself from two pieces:
Piece									Where ours came from
The design formula ~ SV1 + condition					The authors’, copied exactly from their script
The sample table (which sample is which condition, and its SV1 value)	Built by us today (coldata): your mapping table supplied the SRR → library link, and the authors’ sampleTable_SVs.txt supplied each library’s condition and SV1

The authors did the same thing: they passed their sampleTable_SVs.txt and the same formula to DESeq2, and DESeq2 built the matrix. So the recipe is theirs, and the ingredients are their values for our 30 samples.

One small difference: I set the order of the condition levels (PH_intact first). That only changes which group DESeq2 uses as its internal baseline, so it doesn’t affect any of the 4 comparisons, which name both groups explicitly.

PH_intact (the baseline) is the 3 rows with 0 in every condition column: SRR32154025, SRR32154024, and SRR32153999.
Every other sample has exactly one 1, in its own group’s column.
The two lines at the bottom are R’s bookkeeping. assign (0 1 2 2 2 2 2) records which term each column came from: the intercept, SV1, then the 5 condition columns. contr.treatment means each group is compared against the baseline.


The SV1 column shows something important
Region			SV1 range		Sign
PH (14 samples)		−0.262 to −0.022	all negative
POA (16 samples)	+0.006 to +0.265	all positive

SV1 separates the two brain regions perfectly. This is direct evidence for the concern in your progress report that SV1 “may largely separate the two brain regions rather than a technical batch.”

Why it matters: the condition column already tells the model which region each sample is from. So the region part of SV1 is redundant. What SV1 really contributes is the smaller variation within each region, and we don’t know what that variation represents (batch, dissection, RNA quality?). That’s a strong point for the report’s critique section.
